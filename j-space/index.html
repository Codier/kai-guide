🧠 The J-space · a build-up guide

Interpretability, from scratch

What is the “J-space”?

In July 2026 Anthropic published a finding that surprised a lot of people: hidden inside Claude there’s a small, private “workspace” where it does its real thinking — thoughts it never says out loud.

This guide explains that discovery from the ground up. No AI-engineering background assumed. We’ll build every idea you need, one step at a time, and only then explain the finding itself.

◆ The one-line version

Your brain does a huge amount you’re never aware of — balancing your body, parsing grammar — plus a little bit you are aware of, like a thought you can describe or a plan you make on purpose. Anthropic found that Claude has split itself the same way: a small “conscious-access-like” zone (the J-space) floating on an ocean of automatic processing. This guide gets you all the way to understanding why that’s a big deal — and what it isn’t (it does not mean Claude is conscious).

How this guide is organized

Read it in order. Each part is short and assumes only what came before. The first two parts build the mental toolkit; parts 3–4 are the actual discovery; part 5 is why it matters.

The experiments that prove it’s doing real work, not just watching. 5 Why it matters — safety, limits, and the consciousness question Reading a model’s hidden thoughts, catching misbehavior, what others are already replicating, and the honest caveats.
