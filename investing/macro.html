The Hedge & Macro Field Guide

Part 1 valued companies — things with revenue and profit. But BTC, gold, and index funds don't have earnings, so the whole toolkit changes. This is how you think about hedges, crypto, cash, and buying the dip — in plain English.

01 The portfolio as a system

You don't own random things. Each bucket has a job. Balance is the strategy.

A portfolio is a team of players with different roles. You size each role on purpose (a "target allocation"), and when one grows too big or too small versus its target, you rebalance — trim the winner, top up the laggard — to keep the risk where you want it.

BucketIts jobBehaves like
Growth (stocks, crypto)Build wealth over yearsHigh reward, high swings
Hedges (gold, some crypto)Hold value when everything else fallsZig when stocks zag
CashDry powder + safetyFlat, but ready to deploy
Income / index fundsSteady, low-effort market exposureSlow and broad
Drift & rebalancing
If crypto rips +80%, it becomes a bigger slice of the pie than you intended — that's drift. Rebalancing sells a bit of the winner back to target and buys what lagged. It quietly forces "sell high, buy low." (One exception in our rules: crypto has no upper cap, so we let it run and only trim it on its own price ladder — not on drift.)

02 DCA — buying on autopilot

DCA = Dollar-Cost Averaging. Instead of dumping a lump sum in at one price, you buy a fixed dollar amount on a schedule (e.g. every week). Some weeks the price is high, some low — you automatically buy fewer shares when it's expensive and more when it's cheap. Your average cost smooths out.

DCA = same $ amount, fixed schedule, regardless of price

Why it works

  • Kills timing anxiety. You never have to guess the perfect entry — you get the average.
  • Removes emotion. The buy happens whether you feel greedy or scared. Discipline beats mood.
  • Great for volatile assets. The wilder the swings (crypto), the more the "buy more when cheap" effect helps.
The trade-off
Over a long rising market, a lump sum invested early usually beats DCA (more time in the market). DCA's edge is risk control and behavior, not maximum return. We use it as a "learning position" tool — start small on a new name, scale up once the thesis proves out.

03 Valuing things with no earnings

You can't run EV/Sales or P/E on gold — it has no sales. Different asset, different lens.

🥇 Gold
the classic hedge
What drives it
Fear, real interest rates, and the US dollar. When "safe" bonds pay little after inflation, gold shines. When the dollar weakens, gold (priced in dollars) rises.
Its job
Insurance. It usually holds or gains when stocks crash — that's the point, not day-to-day return.
How to "value" it
No cash flow to discount. You judge it by its role (portfolio insurance) and macro backdrop (real yields, dollar), not by a multiple.
₿ Bitcoin & crypto
growth + hedge, split personality
What drives it
Scarcity (fixed supply), adoption (more users/institutions), and liquidity (how much easy money is sloshing around). No earnings — value comes from what others will pay and how many use it.
Split personality
Sometimes it acts like "digital gold" (a hedge); often it trades like a high-octane risk asset (falls hardest in panics). Know which mode the market's in.
How to "value" it
Frameworks (scarcity models, adoption curves, network value) give rough zones, not precise fair values. So we manage it by position size and price ladders, not a target multiple.
Volatility
30–50%+ drawdowns are normal. That's why it's sized deliberately and trimmed on the way up.
📈 Index funds (S&P 500, etc.)
you buy the whole market
What it is
One fund holding hundreds of companies. You're not betting on one stock — you own the average of the whole market.
How to value
You can use an aggregate P/E for the whole index, but the honest answer for most people is "time in the market beats timing." Buy steadily, hold for years.
Its job
Low-effort, diversified growth — the boring backbone that lets you take focused risk elsewhere.

04 Sell ladders for volatile assets

Same idea as the stock trim ladder in Part 1, but for assets with no fair-value anchor you can't set rungs off "value" — so you set them off price and how much you'd regret not selling. As the price climbs into euphoria, you sell pre-set slices, banking profit and reducing risk without ever trying to call the exact top.

Level 1
Price hits first target → sell a small slice, take some risk off
Level 2
Higher target → sell another slice
Level 3
Euphoria zone → trim harder, keep a core "let it ride" stake
The rule that keeps you honest
Only get alerted when price is near a rung — no noise otherwise. And if a rung fires while the crowd is euphoric, you sell into that strength. Same discipline as "trimming into a squeeze" from Part 1: let other people's emotion be your exit liquidity.
Selling at a loss on purpose
Some ladders also have downside triggers — if an asset breaks a level, you cut, even at a loss. That feels awful, but a pre-planned stop is a strategic decision, not an emotional one. The plan protects you from freezing.

05 Buy ladders & the crash fund

Cash isn't dead money — it's dry powder. The plan for a market crash is written before the crash, when you're calm. As the market falls through pre-set levels, you deploy cash in tiers — buying more the deeper it drops.

−8%
Tier 1 → deploy first slice of cash
−15%
Tier 2 → deploy more
−25%+
Tier 3 → back up the truck

Why tiers instead of all at once? Because you never know how deep a crash goes. Tiering guarantees you buy some of the discount without blowing your whole cash pile on the first dip. It's DCA's cousin, aimed at fear instead of a calendar.

06 "Hedges fast, equities slow"

Different assets react to the same shock on different timelines. If a big geopolitical or macro event hits, the insurance-type assets (gold, sometimes crypto, energy/defense) tend to move first and fast — the market reprices fear quickly. The value of the productive companies you own adjusts slower, over quarters, as the real economic effects show up in earnings.

Day 0 — shock hits Fear spikes. Hedges (gold, defense, energy) jump within days.
Weeks 1–4 Risk assets (stocks, crypto) sell off on panic, then start sorting winners from losers.
Months 6–24 The real economic impact slowly shows up in company earnings — equities re-rate gradually.
Why this shapes the plan
Because hedges pay off fast and equities adjust slowly, you want the insurance on before the event (you can't buy it after it's spiked), and you give the equity side a long runway to play out. It's the logic behind holding hedges you hope you never "need."

07 Behavioral traps to watch

Undersizing in fear
When a great asset is down on scary headlines, that's often the best time to add — but fear makes you buy too little. If conviction is real, size it like you mean it.
Chasing the squeeze
Buying because it's spiking. You're paying the forced-buying premium. Ladders sell into that, they don't chase it.
Freezing at a loss
Refusing to execute a planned stop because selling hurts. The pre-written plan exists precisely to override this moment.
Deferring a trim
"It'll go higher, I'll sell later." A rule you don't follow isn't a rule. Fire the ladder when it triggers.
Recency bias
Assuming whatever just happened will keep happening. Bull runs and crashes both end.
Confusing price with value
A higher price isn't proof the thing got better — especially for earning-less assets moved by mood and liquidity.

08 Quick glossary

Allocation
The % of your portfolio in each bucket. Your risk blueprint.
Rebalance
Trim what grew too big, top up what shrank — back to target.
Drift
When a bucket wanders off its target % because it moved a lot.
DCA
Dollar-Cost Averaging — fixed $ bought on a schedule regardless of price.
Hedge
An asset that holds value (or gains) when your main holdings fall. Insurance.
Dry powder
Cash kept ready to buy discounts in a crash.
Drawdown
A peak-to-trough fall. "−40% drawdown" = down 40% from the high.
Volatility
How much the price swings. High = big moves both ways.
Scarcity
Fixed/limited supply — a core reason BTC and gold hold value.
Real yield
Interest rate after inflation. Low/negative real yields help gold.
Risk asset
Something that falls hard in a panic (stocks, most crypto).
Core position
The chunk you hold for the long haul and never fully trim.
Tier / rung
One pre-set price level in a buy or sell ladder.
Time in the market
Staying invested beats trying to jump in/out at perfect times.
